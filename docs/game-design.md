# Game design: a dice-driven streamer career

The creator confirmed an initial 52-week run, one week per turn, satirical Polish-internet tone, **money, viewers, and drama** as the three game statistics, no starting archetypes, event eligibility influenced by all three statistics, and percentage-based consequences for **every choice**. The player chooses **one action at the start of each week**, then may respond to an event. Run length and other numeric defaults are versioned SQLite parameters; numeric tuning belongs in [balance](balance.md).

## Objective and setup

Build a fictional Polish streaming career for up to the configured number of weeks, initially 52. Completing the configured final week without an earlier ending completes the career. Reaching the bankruptcy limit ends it immediately in defeat; selected event outcomes may also cause permanent ban, channel closure, or retirement from streaming. Permanent ban and channel closure are defeats; retirement is neutral. Every ending shows one career score based equally on the final channel audience and net profit earned after expenses, along with final money, the drama path, and notable events. The score compares runs; it does not override the ending type. Calm, mixed, and controversy-driven careers should produce different stories.

The player chooses only a streamer name. The setup screen suggests one by joining two SQLite vocabulary parts; the suggestion can be regenerated or replaced with the player's own name. Name choice has no game effect. See [streamer name generation](streamer-name-generation.md). The first version has no separate channel-name, content-theme, platform, or archetype choice. The same rules and outcomes apply in Polish and English.

## Persistent game state

| Field | Meaning | Rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Week | Current week | Starts at 1; the initial published run ends after week 52, later versions may configure another length |
| Money | Available PLN | Starts at 1,500 in the first published parameters; integer; changes only through categorized cash-flow entries; initially a balance at or below -1,000 ends the run in defeat |
| Viewers | Size of the channel's regular audience | Starts at 20 in the first published parameters; non-negative integer; there is no separate follower or average-live-viewer score |
| Drama | Public controversy level | Starts at 50 in the first published parameters; integer 0–100; low is calmer, high is more controversial |
| Run history | Completed choices, rolls, events, and effects | Stored in the browser save, not the event database |
| RNG, rules, and catalogue versions | Reproducible chance and stable algorithms, parameters, and event rules | Stored in the browser save |

Money, viewers, and drama are the only core numeric game statistics. A description such as “damaged reputation” must translate into changes to these statistics; do not silently reintroduce reputation, trust, energy, equipment level, or a moral score. Event encounter history and temporary narrative flags for story chains are rule state, not extra player-facing statistics. There are no separate one-time viewer milestones in the first version.

## Money and weekly settlement

The player has one money balance. Each change to it is recorded in one of four cash-flow categories: **sponsors, donations, subscriptions, or expenses**. Sponsors, donations, and subscriptions add money; expenses subtract it. The weekly report shows these four subtotals and the net change. They are a breakdown of money, not four additional game statistics.

Subscription revenue is settled **once every week**, including a quiet week, using the run's post-action viewers and versioned rules. There is no separate active-subscriber count in the first version. The proposed amount formula is in [balance](balance.md). Sponsor and donation income occurs through applicable weekly-action or event outcomes. Guaranteed action costs, automatic encounter costs, paid event responses, and random bills are recorded as **separate expense entries**. An outcome can create several entries, for example sponsor income plus a production expense.

The invariant is `closingMoney = openingMoney + sponsors + donations + subscriptions - expenses`, with each subtotal shown as a non-negative PLN amount. Store the underlying signed entries and their source IDs in the browser save so a report can explain a change and a retry cannot pay the same entry twice.

Drama is a **position in public perception**, not a simple health bar. Low drama can unlock calm or cooperative situations, middle drama mixed ones, and high drama scandals and provocative opportunities. Some choices can gain viewers while increasing drama or gain money while losing viewers. The game should not automatically reward the lowest or highest drama in every situation.

## Weekly loop

1. Show the week, three statistics, recent events, and the available choices.
2. The player chooses **one weekly action**. The creator accepted an ordinary stream, a provocative stunt, a sponsor pitch, and a quiet week as the working first menu. Their costs and odds in [balance](balance.md) remain subject to testing. Every offered action shows its cost and an honest summary of possible consequences.
3. A paid action can be selected without cash on hand, even if its guaranteed cost alone temporarily reaches or crosses the bankruptcy limit. Record that cost as an expense, then resolve the action using recorded percentage chances and one seeded dice roll. The result can add categorized cash flows and change viewers or drama. Settle this week's subscription income once after the action. **Only then** check the reconciled balance; the outcome and subscription payment can rescue the run. If the balance is still at or below the bankruptcy limit, finish in defeat without checking events.
4. Check events against the current money (including that week's subscription payout), viewers, drama band, week, narrative flags, and repeat policies. A follow-up from an earlier choice must still pass its own encounter roll. A week may present **up to three events** under the first published `MaxEventsPerWeek` parameter. Roll an eligible event at most once during that week; select uniformly among events whose encounter rolls passed, without priority for a chain follow-up. If no event passes, or the cap is reached, complete the week. Charge the selected event's **automatic encounter cost** before presenting its choices. This is a separate checkpoint: if it reaches the bankruptcy limit, finish in defeat without a response roll. Other eligible events do not charge anything.
5. Otherwise, the player chooses a response. An **additional response cost** may be funded by debt, even if that cost alone temporarily crosses the bankruptcy limit. Record it as an expense, roll once against that response's weighted outcomes, and apply exactly one result before checking the reconciled balance. The result may change money, viewers, drama, and browser-local narrative flags, or trigger one of the three special early endings. If the final balance is at or below the limit, bankruptcy is the primary ending reason even if the same result also names a special ending. Otherwise show the special-ending recap if one applies. If the run remains active, return to event selection with the updated state, excluding events already shown that week and never rerolling an event already rolled that week. A follow-up flag set in this event cannot unlock its sequel until a later week.
6. Write each pending event and interim result to browser autosave. After the final event of the week, save the completed week and show one report covering the action and all events before the next turn. The player may also save to one of three manual slots, export a save file, or import a validated save file after confirming the destination slot. If a checkpoint ends the career, save the terminal state and show that ending's recap instead of another event or next-turn action.

Every player decision with uncertain consequences has a probability distribution. A “safe” choice may have a high chance of a modest result, while a risky choice may offer a larger upside and a meaningful downside. The final outcome is not chosen by the UI. The exact weekly action menu and odds will be tuned through the first playable slice.

## Events and choices

Events have typed conditions, including a **calm**, **middle**, or **high-drama** band, minimum/maximum viewers, and minimum/maximum money. An event can target one band or several. The authored catalogue should contain events specifically for each band and events available across bands. An event may be marked once per career or repeatable after its own cooldown. A follow-up additionally requires the narrative flag produced by an earlier rolled result and may have a limited window of later weeks in which it can appear. Eligibility is checked before an encounter roll, including for follow-ups. Choice outcomes have separate percentage chances; seeing an event does not imply its risky result will happen.

The content and odds should produce at least one event in **most non-terminal weeks** of representative full careers, while preserving the chance of a quiet week. Some weeks may have two or more events. This is a playtest target rather than a forced event each week. A chained event competes with other eligible events under the same seeded selection rule; the chain can end without a follow-up if its roll never succeeds before its eligibility window closes.

Both cost types are optional. An automatic encounter cost is unavoidable once that event is selected and may put money below zero or trigger bankruptcy; authoring conditions can restrict an event if that would be unfair. A paid response may be selected with insufficient cash, and its guaranteed cost can temporarily cross the bankruptcy limit because the response's rolled outcome is settled in the same atomic step. The response can rescue or bankrupt the run at that checkpoint. Keep at least one free, selectable response if the event survives its encounter cost. The encounter cost and response cost are displayed separately and applied at most once, including when a pending-event request is retried.

The creator is considering **roughly 200–400 distinct events for the first public release**; the exact count remains open. The initial documentation contains [20 Polish draft examples](sample-events.md); a smaller representative subset covering each drama band, viewer threshold, money threshold, and outcome shape is enough to build and test the engine before the release catalogue is complete. Each published event needs stable IDs, Polish and English text, at least two meaningful responses, explicit probabilities, and effects using only the three statistics. Localizing text must not change odds, IDs, eligibility, or effects. See [event system](event-system.md) and [event catalogue](event-catalogue.md).

Example design theme: a fan sends a private message. Responses include ignoring it, replying politely, or flirting. A reply may lead to a pleasant adult interaction, an impersonator publishing the messages, or an age concern that ends contact immediately. The consequences are expressed as viewer, money, and drama changes. The story does not develop romantic or sexual content involving a minor, and impersonation is the harmful act rather than a person's gender presentation. This is a design example; exact copy, odds, and effects require content review.

Events may sharply allude to recognizable Polish internet stories about public creators while using fictional personal names, brands, and platforms. Their risks, consequences, and comic framing are written as game fiction; each identifiable allusion needs review before publication. Private people must not be recognizable subjects of these allusions. There are no additional blanket topic exclusions; review sensitive themes case by case within the existing boundaries in [experience and content](experience-and-content.md).

## Progression and ending

The creator confirmed an **automatic defeat at a balance of -1,000 PLN or less** for the initial published parameters, including exactly -1,000 PLN. The threshold is a typed, versioned field in the shared SQLite game catalogue; an active run keeps its published catalogue version. Check the balance after each atomic step: (1) the weekly action, its rolled outcome, and that week's subscription settlement together; (2) **each** selected event's automatic encounter cost; (3) **each** chosen response's guaranteed cost and rolled outcome together. A guaranteed player-selected cost may cross the limit inside its step; an outcome can offset it before the check. An automatic encounter cost is its own step and cannot be rescued by a response. Cash entries inside a step reconcile before the check, so their order cannot change the ending. Once defeated, no more choices, event rolls, or weekly settlements occur. If bankruptcy happens during the configured final week, defeat takes precedence over ordinary completion. If a response both reaches bankruptcy and carries a special ending reason, bankruptcy is primary; retain the full rolled outcome and cash entries in the history.

Completion, bankruptcy, and special-ending recaps show the single career score, its audience and net-profit components, money ledger totals, drama path, and choices made. A bankruptcy recap is clearly labelled as a loss even if its score is high. Net profit excludes that run's pinned starting balance, initially 1,500 PLN; drama has no direct score bonus or penalty. The confirmed first formula and reference values are in [balance](balance.md). Viewer thresholds can unlock ordinary events; no separate milestone reward or event slot interrupts the weekly event rolls. Do not add an invented reputation or community score.

## Information and fairness

- Show guaranteed costs, possible outcomes, and their percentages before confirmation. Do not hide a severe result behind a vague “small risk” label.
- Use a seeded, versioned random algorithm so the same state, catalogue, and choices produce the same outcome.
- Apply one outcome per choice and record the roll, outcome ID, and numeric deltas. Retries must not roll again.
- Keep losses bounded so a single unlucky event does not erase a healthy run without a clear, deliberately accepted risk.
- Let calm, mixed, and controversial paths all produce interesting events and viable endings. Human playtests decide whether those paths feel different and fair.
